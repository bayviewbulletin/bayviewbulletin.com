Groppi's: the 1913 Italian grocery that got a second life

For generations, the corner of E. Russell and S. Wentworth avenues meant one thing to Bay View's Italian families: Groppi's. The Groppi family, Italian immigrants, opened their grocery there in 1913, and their twelve children all worked in the store, according to the Wisconsin Historical Society's property record.

The building is older than the store. The historical society says it began around 1900 as a double workers' cottage built by the Milwaukee Iron Co., the rolling mill company Bay View grew up around, and the cottage can still be seen at the back. A brick storefront went on in 1923 and an addition in 1924. It counts as a contributing building in the Bay View Historic District, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1982, and the society describes it as the only Italian grocery on Milwaukee's south side.

In February 2003, Tom Groppi, the last of the Groppi children to run it, closed the store. That November it reopened under John Nehring and Anne Finch-Nehring, who owned other Milwaukee-area groceries. They kept the Groppi name and added a deli, a fresh meat counter and a small bar in the back, according to Spectrum News and Urban Milwaukee.

The store changed hands again in January 2024, when brothers Ryan and Casey Foltz took over, leasing the building and keeping the G. Groppi name with Tom Groppi's blessing, Urban Milwaukee reported. Casey Foltz also owns Puddler's Hall down the street, which serves the market's paninis.
