Bay View history · This month in history · 1908

St. Augustine's steeple rises over Howell Avenue

In November 1908, Bay View's German-speaking Catholics moved into the building that still anchors Howell Avenue. St. Augustine of Hippo Church was dedicated on Thanksgiving Day, Nov. 26, 1908, by Milwaukee Archbishop Sebastian Messmer, a little more than seven months after its cornerstone was laid on Palm Sunday.

The parish was barely two decades old. Around 47 families organized it in 1887 so they could hear sermons in German without traveling across town, and they bought lots at Howell Avenue and Graham Street. (The parish's own history lists 1887 as its founding, while its first pastor, the Rev. Peter Schumacher, was appointed and first Mass said in 1888.) For nearly 20 years the congregation worshipped upstairs in a combined church and school at Homer and Graham streets, with classrooms below.

In December 1906 parishioners voted to build a proper church for no more than $36,000. They hired Brust & Philipp, a prominent Milwaukee firm, whose Romanesque revival design in cream brick gave the neighborhood the tall steeple, rose window and triple arched entrances still visible today. Three days after the dedication, the Blessed Sacrament was carried in procession from the old church to the new one.

The building kept filling in over the years: a pipe organ in 1910, three steeple bells in 1914, side and main altars in the 1920s and '30s. During World War I the parish quietly dropped German from its bulletins and sermons. By 1973 it counted roughly 1,300 families, and the four-faced steeple clock added in 2006 has become a Howell Avenue landmark of its own.

St. Augustine is still an active parish at 2530 S. Howell Ave., and it opens its doors each fall for Doors Open Milwaukee and its Oktoberfest, a nod to those German founders.
