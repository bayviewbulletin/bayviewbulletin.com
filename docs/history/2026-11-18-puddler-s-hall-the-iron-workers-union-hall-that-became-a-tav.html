Puddler's Hall, the iron workers' union hall that became a tavern

The dark green building with maroon trim at 2461 S. St. Clair St. started out as a union hall. Iron workers at the Milwaukee Iron Company's rolling mill, the puddlers and boilers who worked molten iron into bars and rails, put it up in the early 1870s. Urban Milwaukee dates it to 1872, while Milwaukee Record says 1873. The workers used it for meetings, concerts and celebrations.

Those were the years when Bay View was a mill town. In May 1886, thousands of Milwaukee workers struck for an eight-hour day, and the march on the Bay View mill ended on May 5 when the National Guard opened fire, killing seven people. It remains the bloodiest event in Wisconsin's labor history. Today three signs above the tavern's front windows repeat the movement's slogan: eight hours for work, eight hours for sleep, eight hours for what you will.

In the early 1890s the union sold the hall to the Falk, Jung and Borchert brewery, and it became a tied house selling one brewer's beer. When that brewery was absorbed by Pabst, it became a Pabst tavern. Around the start of Prohibition it passed to the Barbieri family, who added a dance hall in back. Barbieri's Dance Hall ran until 1979. Milwaukee Record notes it was rumored to have been a speakeasy.

After that came a string of names, including Mardee's Party and Sue's Bay View Bandwagon. In 2002 a new owner renamed it Puddler's Hall to honor the building's roots and hung photos of the mill and early Bay View. Casey Foltz, a Bay View neighbor, reopened it in 2011 after a short closure. Milwaukee Record counts it as the city's second-oldest tavern, behind only the Landmark Inn near the airport.

It is still a neighborhood gathering place. The Bay View Table Tennis Club plays there on Thursdays, and the Milwaukee Guitar Club meets there too.
