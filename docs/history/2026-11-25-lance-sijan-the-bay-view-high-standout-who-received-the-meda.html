Lance Sijan: the Bay View High standout who received the Medal of Honor

Lance Peter Sijan grew up in Bay View and was a standout at Bay View High School: senior class president, an all-city end on the football team, and the lead in a school production of "The King and I." He went on to the Air Force Academy, graduated in 1965, and by July 1967 was flying F-4 Phantom missions in the Vietnam War.

On Nov. 9, 1967, his jet went down over Laos. Rescue crews made voice contact with him and hovered overhead, but he was badly injured and warned them off because enemy troops were all around him. Back in Bay View, two Air Force officers came to his family's door with the news that he was missing.

Sijan evaded capture for 46 days, surviving on plants and insects despite a skull fracture, a mangled hand and a broken leg. He was captured on Christmas Day 1967 and taken to a prison camp in Hanoi, where he died on Jan. 22, 1968, at 25. His family didn't learn his fate until 1974, when his remains came home; he is buried in Arlington Park Cemetery in Greenfield.

Fellow prisoners later described his courage, and in 1976 President Gerald Ford posthumously awarded him the Medal of Honor. The Air Force Academy named a dormitory Sijan Hall, the Air Force named its top leadership award for him, and in Bay View, the football field where he practiced was renamed Sijan Park.
