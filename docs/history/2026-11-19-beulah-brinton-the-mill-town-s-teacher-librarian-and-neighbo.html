Beulah Brinton, the mill town's teacher, librarian and neighbor

In 1872, Beulah Brinton arrived in Bay View from Michigan with her husband, Warren, who took a job at the Milwaukee Iron Company's rolling mill. The mill was founded by her cousin, Eber Brock Ward. The couple settled into a frame house at what is now 2590 S. Superior St., built for them in 1870 and 1871.

Bay View was then a company town full of newly arrived iron workers and their families. Brinton turned her home into a gathering place. She taught English and reading to mill workers' wives and children, along with sewing, cooking and music, and sometimes worked as a midwife. She raised money for books, which mill owner Ward matched, and lent them from her house. That collection of some 300 volumes is counted as Bay View's first public library. It later moved to the first branch of the Milwaukee Public Library and became the core of today's Bay View library.

She was a writer, too. She published a Civil War novel, "Man Is Love," in the 1870s, edited a local paper, the Bay View Herald, and preached at her Methodist church. She is credited with bringing tennis to the neighborhood, with a court on the empty lot beside her house. After the 1886 shootings at the mill during the eight-hour strike, she led prayer gatherings to help the neighborhood heal.

Warren Brinton died in 1895, and Beulah later moved to Chicago to live with her son. While she was away, the historical society says, she rented the house to a family whose son grew up to be the actor Spencer Tracy. In 1924 the city named Bay View's first social center, in an old firehouse on St. Clair Street, after her. She came home to Bay View after a stroke in 1926 and died in March 1928 at about 92. Sources differ on whether she was born in 1835 or 1836.

The Beulah Brinton Community Center on Bay Street, opened in 1981, still carries her name. Her house is now the headquarters of the Bay View Historical Society, and the lot beside it, where she once played tennis, is still empty.
