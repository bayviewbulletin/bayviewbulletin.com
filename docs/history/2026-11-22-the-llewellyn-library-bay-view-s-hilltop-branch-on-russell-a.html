The Llewellyn Library: Bay View's hilltop branch on Russell Avenue

For its first decades, Bay View's library kept moving. MPL dates the neighborhood's library to Beulah Brinton's lending shelves of the 1870s; after Bay View joined Milwaukee in 1887, its library became the Milwaukee Public Library's first branch, at what is now 2457 S. Wentworth Ave. (today the home of Goodkind). It later moved twice along Kinnickinnic Avenue, in about 1896 and again in 1908.

In 1913, Silas J. and John T. Llewellyn donated a lot on Russell Avenue in honor of their father, Henry Llewellyn. The Henry Llewellyn Branch opened there in 1914, built with a $30,000 city bond and designed by Milwaukee architects Van Ryn & DeGelleke in a Neoclassical style. The Wisconsin Historical Society calls it the first neighborhood library the city built for its system. It sat on a rise above the street, reached by a grand stair, and opened with fewer than 1,000 books.

By 1958 the collection topped 10,000 volumes and had nowhere to go. A $150,000 remodeling designed by Grassold & Johnson opened in 1959: crews removed the grand stair, dug away the hill to turn the basement into the main floor, and added a modern one-story wing across the front. The library ran from the former Bay View Baptist Church next door during the work.

The branch served Bay View until 1993, when the current library opened in its wedge-shaped building at 2566 S. Kinnickinnic Ave. The old building was leased to Milwaukee Public Schools, which used it for Bay View High School's Redcat Academy, then sat empty. The city put it up for sale in 2024, and this fall it picked a developer to turn it into apartments. The name "Henry Llewellyn Branch Library" is still carved in the stone frieze.
