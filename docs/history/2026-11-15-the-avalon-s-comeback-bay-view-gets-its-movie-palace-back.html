The Avalon's comeback: Bay View gets its movie palace back

In November 2014, Bay View learned the date it had waited years for: the Avalon Theater at 2473 S. Kinnickinnic Ave. would reopen in December. The 1929 movie house had been closed since 2000, and Urban Milwaukee toured the renovation that month and then reported the grand-opening plans.

The Avalon opened at the dawn of talking pictures, designed by Russell Barr Williamson, an architect who had trained under Frank Lloyd Wright. It was an "atmospheric" theater, built to make moviegoers feel they were sitting outdoors in old-world Spain, with Spanish-style walls and a ceiling of twinkling lights. It was one of five atmospheric theaters built in the Milwaukee area, and the only one left.

Lee Barczak of the Neighborhood Theater Group bought the shuttered building in 2005 for $1.1 million, but the Great Recession stalled his plans. The $2.5 million restoration that followed rebuilt the starry ceiling with 1,800 LED lights mapped from the night sky over Granada, Spain, up from about 150 bulbs, and added a kitchen and big two-person seats with tables so patrons could eat during the show. Barczak told Urban Milwaukee the balcony, where he said he had his first kiss as a teenager, would stay closed for now.

Preview nights in mid-December doubled as a fundraiser for the Bay View Historical Society, and the public grand opening came on Dec. 18, 2014, with The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies. The Avalon is still on KK today, mixing new releases with pop-up markets and movie nights.
