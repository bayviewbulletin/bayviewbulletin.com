The Hoan Bridge: Bay View's 'Bridge to Nowhere'

The big steel arch that carries I-794 over the harbor mouth between downtown and Bay View grew out of 1960s freeway plans. City leaders had long wanted a high bridge along the lakefront, and the federal highway act of 1956 promised to pay most of the bill if it became part of the interstate system. First called the Harbor Bridge, it was soon named for Daniel Hoan, Milwaukee's longtime Socialist mayor.

Construction began in 1970, and accounts agree the main span was essentially done by about 1973 or 1974. But the freeways meant to feed it were in trouble. Opposition to a "ring of concrete" around downtown, and the fight against the Lake Freeway that would have carried traffic south along the lakefront, halted the connecting roads. With little to connect to, the finished bridge picked up a nickname that stuck for years: the "Bridge to Nowhere."

About $22 million in federal money finally completed the ramps, and the Hoan opened to traffic in 1977, according to Milwaukee Magazine. Its south end touched down at the edge of Bay View, and the Lake Parkway later extended the route farther south toward the suburbs.

The bridge has had a dramatic life since. In December 2000, two of the three main girders on one lakefront approach span cracked and the northbound deck sagged, closing those lanes for months; no one was hurt. A rehabilitation finished around 2016 was meant to add decades to its life, and since 2020 the arch has glowed at night with privately funded LED lights. This week it's in the news again: the Rethink 794 group's new boulevard plan for downtown keeps the Hoan connection.
