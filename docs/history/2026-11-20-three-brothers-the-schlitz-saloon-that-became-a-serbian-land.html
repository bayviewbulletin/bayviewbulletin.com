Three Brothers: the Schlitz saloon that became a Serbian landmark

The little cream city brick building at 2414 S. St. Clair St. began as a beer outlet, not a restaurant. In July 1897 the Joseph Schlitz Brewing Company bought the small triangular lot from a local couple for $250, and on July 26 it applied for a permit to build a tavern there. Milwaukee architect Charles Kirchhoff, who drew plans for several Schlitz taverns around the city, designed it in the Queen Anne style, at an estimated cost of $5,000.

It was a "tied house": the brewery owned the building and leased it to an operator who could pour only Schlitz. The giveaway is still on the roof, where a corner turret is topped by a belted globe, the brewery's trademark. Around 1920, when breweries were pushed to sell off property unrelated to making beer, most tied houses like this one passed to private owners.

The building's second life began in the 1950s. Milun Radicevic, a Serbian veteran of World War II who came to Milwaukee in 1949, had been running a tavern on South 2nd Street since 1953. He moved the business to St. Clair Street and named it Three Brothers for his three sons. His wife, Milunka, created the original menu of Serbian home cooking, much of it made from scratch. The family and its website date the move to 1956; the 1976 National Register nomination says the owners bought the building in 1958.

Recognition followed. The city named the tavern a Milwaukee landmark in 1976, and it was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on April 11, 1977, as the Joseph Schlitz Brewing Company Saloon. In 2002 Three Brothers won a James Beard Foundation America's Classics award. A granddaughter told OnMilwaukee the place was "our family's Ellis Island."

Nearly 130 years after Schlitz built it, the turret and globe still mark the corner, and the third generation of the family still runs the restaurant.
