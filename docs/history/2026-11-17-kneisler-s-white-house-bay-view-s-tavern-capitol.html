Kneisler's White House, Bay View's tavern "Capitol"

For more than a century, the white wooden building with the pointed turrets at 2900 S. Kinnickinnic Ave., where KK bends at Ellen Street, was the tavern Bay View called the White House. William Kneisler, whose family had run groceries in Bay View, opened it in the early 1890s, with money from the Schlitz Brewing Co. Schlitz loans like that usually came with a catch: the bar could pour only Schlitz beer.

The sources don't agree on the exact year. The tavern's sign said it was established in 1891, and OnMilwaukee cites both an 1890 opening-day photo and an 1891 opening. The Wisconsin Historical Society's building survey and the city's 2018 landmark study give 1893 as the construction date, and a city preservation staffer said records don't back up 1891. Either way, the Queen Anne saloon, with clapboard walls on a cream city brick foundation, is one of Milwaukee's older surviving taverns.

Kneisler's was more than a bar. Accounts describe rented rooms upstairs, a barber in the basement, early telephone and telegraph service and a stop for stagecoaches. Local politicians drank there and election results were chalked on a board, which is how it picked up the nicknames "The Capitol" and the White House. Kneisler soon paid off the brewery loan, and after his death in 1919 his nephew Herb ran the tavern until the 1970s. The family held the property until the mid-2000s.

The tavern closed at the end of 2017. When a plan to cut big new windows into the facade surfaced, the building was nominated for protection, and the city made it a historic landmark in 2018. A restaurant has since opened inside, behind the same turrets.
