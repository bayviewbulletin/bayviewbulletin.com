Bay View history

One story from Bay View’s past every day: the shops, taverns, factories, people and places that made the neighborhood. Newest first.

1967 · Posted Wednesday, November 25, 2026

Lance Sijan: the Bay View High standout who received the Medal of Honor

In November 1967, Air Force Capt. Lance Sijan, a Bay View High graduate, was shot down over Laos. His courage as a prisoner earned him the Medal of Honor.

1949 · Posted Tuesday, November 24, 2026

DeMarinis: Bay View's pizza family since 1949

In 1949 Vincent and Lucille DeMarinis opened a tavern at Wentworth and Potter. Within two years it was an Italian kitchen, and the DeMarinis name still means pizza in Bay View.

1918 · Posted Monday, November 23, 2026

Armistice week, 1918: 10,000 Bay View marchers fill Humboldt Park

In November 1918, days after the armistice, more than 10,000 Bay View residents marched down KK and Logan to Humboldt Park to honor the neighborhood's soldiers.

1914 · Posted Sunday, November 22, 2026

The Llewellyn Library: Bay View's hilltop branch on Russell Avenue

In 1914, Bay View got its first purpose-built library: the Henry Llewellyn Branch on Russell Avenue, on land donated by Llewellyn's sons. It served the neighborhood until 1993.

1977 · Posted Saturday, November 21, 2026

The Hoan Bridge: Bay View's 'Bridge to Nowhere'

Built in the early 1970s, the Hoan Bridge sat finished but unconnected for years, mocked as the 'Bridge to Nowhere,' before it finally opened to traffic in 1977.

1897 · Posted Friday, November 20, 2026

Three Brothers: the Schlitz saloon that became a Serbian landmark

In 1897 Schlitz built a turreted tavern on St. Clair Street. Six decades later a Serbian family turned it into Three Brothers, still serving today.

1872 · Posted Thursday, November 19, 2026

Beulah Brinton, the mill town's teacher, librarian and neighbor

In 1872, Beulah Brinton moved into a new house on Superior Street and opened it to the rolling mill's immigrant families. Her lending library grew into Milwaukee's first branch library.

1873 · Posted Wednesday, November 18, 2026

Puddler's Hall, the iron workers' union hall that became a tavern

In the early 1870s, Bay View's iron puddlers built a union hall on St. Clair Street. More than 150 years later, Puddler's Hall is still pouring.

1891 · Posted Tuesday, November 17, 2026

Kneisler's White House, Bay View's tavern "Capitol"

In the early 1890s, William Kneisler opened a turreted tavern at KK and Ellen. For a century, Kneisler's White House was where Bay View talked politics.

1908 · Posted Monday, November 16, 2026

St. Augustine's steeple rises over Howell Avenue

In November 1908, Bay View's German Catholics dedicated St. Augustine of Hippo Church on Howell Avenue, the Brust & Philipp landmark still standing 118 years later.

2014 · Posted Sunday, November 15, 2026

The Avalon's comeback: Bay View gets its movie palace back

In November 2014, after 14 dark years and a $2.5 million restoration, the 1929 Avalon Theater on KK announced its reopening. It's still showing movies today.

2003 · Posted Saturday, November 14, 2026

Groppi's: the 1913 Italian grocery that got a second life

In November 2003, months after the last Groppi closed it, the 90-year-old Italian grocery at Russell and Wentworth reopened under new owners. It's still there.

1887 · Posted Friday, November 13, 2026

Bay View governed itself as a village for eight years before its roughly 4,000 residents v…

Bay View governed itself as a village for eight years before its roughly 4,000 residents voted in 1887 to join Milwaukee, becoming the city's 17th Ward. The trade gave up independence for city services residents wanted, such as sewers, sidewalks and street lights. The Beulah Brinton House, pictured, was an early gathering place for the village and is now home to the Bay View Historical Society.

1882 · Posted Thursday, November 12, 2026

The North Chicago Rolling Mill (the iron works Bay View grew up around) as it looked in 18…

The North Chicago Rolling Mill (the iron works Bay View grew up around) as it looked in 1882. The village of Bay View had incorporated three years earlier with 2,592 people as Milwaukee's first suburb, and residents voted to join the city in 1887.
