1891 · Posted Tuesday, November 17, 2026

Kneisler's White House, Bay View's tavern "Capitol"

In the early 1890s, William Kneisler opened a turreted tavern at KK and Ellen. For a century, Kneisler's White House was where Bay View talked politics.

1908 · Posted Monday, November 16, 2026

St. Augustine's steeple rises over Howell Avenue

In November 1908, Bay View's German Catholics dedicated St. Augustine of Hippo Church on Howell Avenue, the Brust & Philipp landmark still standing 118 years later.

2014 · Posted Sunday, November 15, 2026

The Avalon's comeback: Bay View gets its movie palace back

In November 2014, after 14 dark years and a $2.5 million restoration, the 1929 Avalon Theater on KK announced its reopening. It's still showing movies today.

2003 · Posted Saturday, November 14, 2026

Groppi's: the 1913 Italian grocery that got a second life

In November 2003, months after the last Groppi closed it, the 90-year-old Italian grocery at Russell and Wentworth reopened under new owners. It's still there.

1887 · Posted Friday, November 13, 2026

Bay View governed itself as a village for eight years before its roughly 4,000 residents voted in 1887 to join Milwaukee, becoming the city's 17th Ward. The trade gave up independence for city services residents wanted, such as sewers, sidewalks and street lights. The Beulah Brinton House, pictured, was an early gathering place for the village and is now home to the Bay View Historical Society.

The North Chicago Rolling Mill (the iron works Bay View grew up around) as it looked in 1882. The village of Bay View had incorporated three years earlier with 2,592 people as Milwaukee's first suburb, and residents voted to join the city in 1887.
