How Milwaukee decides which lead water lines get replaced first

Milwaukee Water Works still had more than 55,000 residential properties with lead service lines at the end of 2025, plus about 27,000 whose pipe material is unknown, and faces a federal 2037 deadline to replace them. A Wisconsin Watch report published by Milwaukee NNS explains how the utility picks where crews go next.

Each property gets a score: 70% from a UW-Madison measure of neighborhood socioeconomic disadvantage, 25% from local child lead-poisoning rates and 5% from the density of lead lines nearby. Scores are updated every other year. The utility has sped up from about 1,000 replacements a year to 5,000, with priority work so far concentrated on the North Side and expanding into near South Side neighborhoods.

The city expects to pass 20,000 total replacements next year, but federal infrastructure money runs out in 2028, and officials may have to go back to asking homeowners to pay part of the cost. Bay View's many pre-1962 homes are among those that may still have lead lines; residents can look up their address on Milwaukee Water Works' lead service line map.
