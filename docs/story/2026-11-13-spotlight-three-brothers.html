Spotlight: Three Brothers

On a quiet residential corner of Bay View, Three Brothers has served Serbian home cooking at 2414 S. St. Clair St. since 1956, which makes 2026 its 70th year. The cream city brick building went up around the turn of the last century as a Schlitz tied house, and the brewery's belted globe still crowns its turret. Much of that era survives inside: the original bar, a Grand Tetons mural behind it, bentwood chairs and Formica-topped tables.

Founder Milun Radicevic, a Serbian army veteran who survived imprisonment during World War II, came to Milwaukee in 1949 and ran a tavern on S. 2nd Street before buying this building. He named the restaurant for his three sons, who were still in Yugoslavia at the time, and his wife, Milunka, built the menu from her own recipes. Their son Branko later left a banking career in California to help when his father fell ill, and Branko's wife, Patricia, learned the recipes at her mother-in-law's side and took over the kitchen in 1982. Branko died in 2015. Patricia and her children carry it on today, and only family members have ever done the cooking.

The menu has barely changed in decades. The star is burek, flaky layers of paper-thin filo filled with beef, cheese, or spinach and cheese; it takes a while to bake, so regulars call ahead to order it. Other staples include sarma (stuffed cabbage), chicken paprikash, goulash with potato dumplings, chevapchichi sausages, raznjici pork skewers, roast lamb, duck and even goose, with house-made desserts like strudel, baklava and a seven-layer walnut torte. Given a day and a half's notice, the kitchen can adapt dishes for vegetarian, vegan or gluten-free diners.

The accolades go back a long way. The James Beard Foundation named Three Brothers an America's Classic in 2002, and the city designated the building a Milwaukee landmark in 1976. The restaurant has turned up in Gourmet, Bon Appétit and The New York Times, and in 2025 USA Today put it on its national Restaurants of the Year list.

If you go, plan ahead: reservations are recommended, and the restaurant takes cash or checks only (there's an ATM next door at DeMarinis). With holiday gatherings coming up, it's worth calling early if you have your heart set on burek or a roast.

Three Brothers, 2414 S. St. Clair St. Open Wednesday to Friday 5 to 9 p.m. and Saturday and Sunday 4 to 9 p.m.; closed Monday and Tuesday. Call (414) 481-7530, visit threebrothersmke.com or follow @threebrothersmke on Instagram.
