MPS chief: a $58 million deficit and fewer students than expected

Milwaukee Public Schools Superintendent Brenda Cassellius used her first State of Our Schools address on Thursday at Discovery World to lay out a tough budget picture, under the theme "stability first, excellence next."

According to WPR, the district's deficit has grown to about $58 million, up from the $46 million gap the board tried to close with cuts in February. Enrollment came in at 58,088 students, about 2,200 below projections, which means less state aid.

WISN reported that she offered no timeline for another referendum. She pointed to roughly 300 central-office jobs cut, a small gain in grade 3-8 reading scores on the Forward Exam (23.6% to 25.1%) and a drop in student gun deaths from 23 to 12. State report cards are due next week, and the board is nearing a vote on a long-range facilities plan that would close and merge several schools, which matters for every MPS family in Bay View.
