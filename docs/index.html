Good morning, Bay View, and welcome to the very first Bulletin. Today there's a last call for a free community meal that's been feeding neighbors since 1994, a Thursday night show at Cactus Club, and Milwaukee's newest Cy Young winner. Coffee's on.

Top stories

  • Bread and Jam's free community meal ends its current run with a Thanksgiving feast

    Bay View United Methodist Church (2772 S. Kinnickinnic Ave.) will serve its last free community meal in its current form on Sunday, Nov. 29, from 2:30 to 3:30 p.m., with a traditional Thanksgiving feast. The church's Bread and Jam weekly meal has fed neighbors since 1994, with food from Feeding America and volunteer cooks and servers from across Bay View and the South Side. Questions go to Pastor Anna-Lisa Hunter at the church.

    CommunityMilwaukee Neighborhood News Service · Nov. 12

  • Mayor's deadline for budget vetoes is Tuesday, and Bay View's street-light fix is in the mix

    The Common Council's amended 2027 budget is on Mayor Cavalier Johnson's desk. He has until close of business Tuesday, Nov. 17, to sign it or issue vetoes. It includes our alder Marina Dimitrijevic's amendment adding money for library hours and roads, restoring electrician jobs, and creating a hiring bonus aimed at faster street-light repairs. It also includes a $1.35 million study of a possible city takeover of We Energies.

    City HallUrban Milwaukee · Nov. 6, deadline Nov. 17

Streets & parking

  • Winter parking rules start Dec. 1: check your block now

    From Dec. 1 to March 1, there's no overnight parking on through highways and bus routes, and that includes Kinnickinnic Avenue. During a declared snow emergency, alternate-side parking is required citywide from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. Fines escalate from $50 to $100 to $150, and repeat offenders can be towed. Sign up for the city's winter parking text alerts before the first storm.

    ParkingCity of Milwaukee DPW · Evergreen

Milwaukee top 10

The biggest stories from the rest of the city.

  1. The Miz is the NL Cy Young winner, unanimously

    The Brewers' Jacob Misiorowski won the National League Cy Young Award Wednesday night in a unanimous vote, capping his first full season in the majors. That season ended with Milwaukee's first World Series title.

    SportsDrydenWire / AP

  2. We Energies rate hike approved, but smaller than requested

    State regulators approved a smaller two-year increase than the utility had sought, its sixth hike in six years. The Common Council then voted 15-0 to fund a study of a city takeover of the utility, which still needs the mayor's approval.

    GovernmentWisconsin Watch via Milwaukee NNS

  3. MPS State of Our Schools address at Discovery World

    Superintendent Cassellius is laying out the district's progress and priorities this morning to community, civic and business leaders.

    SchoolsWISN 12

  4. MPS school closures: one community meeting left

    A post-election push for more state funding could change the math. The final listening session is Monday, Nov. 16, at North Division High School.

    SchoolsWUWM

  5. Wisconsin Supreme Court to hear the Act 10 appeal

    The state's highest court will take up the challenge to the 2011 law limiting most public workers' collective bargaining. An appeals court upheld the law in July.

    GovernmentDrydenWire

  6. Homicide charge in the death of a longtime MPS teacher

    Prosecutors charged a 22-year-old with first-degree intentional homicide in the Nov. 5 death of a special education teacher and Roosevelt football coach near 12th and Walnut.

    Public safetyTMJ4

  7. Domestic violence high-risk caseload has more than doubled

    Milwaukee's Domestic Violence High Risk Team, run through Sojourner Family Peace Center, staffed 1,685 cases in 2025, up 113% from 2019. If you or someone you know needs help, Sojourner's 24-hour hotline is (414) 933-2722.

Bay View history moment

1882

The North Chicago Rolling Mill (the iron works Bay View grew up around) as it looked in 1882. The village of Bay View had incorporated three years earlier with 2,592 people as Milwaukee's first suburb, and residents voted to join the city in 1887.
