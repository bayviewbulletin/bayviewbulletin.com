Short-term rentals in Milwaukee: the rules, and how to report a problem

Airbnb and Vrbo stays shorter than a month need a state license that the city inspects. Here's how it works and who to call.

Renting out a room or a whole home to visitors for less than a month counts as a "tourist rooming house" under Wisconsin law, whether it's booked through Airbnb, Vrbo or anywhere else. The operator needs a state license, and in Milwaukee the city's Department of Neighborhood Services (DNS) handles it for the state: owners send an application with a floor plan, pay the fee DNS bills them, and pass an inspection before the license is issued.

The city has limited power over short-term rentals beyond that, but licensing and the city's registration process are how it keeps track of who is responsible for a property. When DNS finds an unlicensed rental, it can order the owner to get licensed by a deadline, as it did after the Nov. 15 shooting at a party at an unlicensed rental on S. Burrell Street, and require the property to register with the city.

If you think a home on your block is being rented short-term without a license, or a rental is causing problems, you can now report it through the city's online service request system, MKE Mobile (city.milwaukee.gov/ucc/mkemobile). Ald. Marina Dimitrijevic's office asked for that option after the Burrell Street shooting. For anything happening right now, such as a party that's out of control, noise, fights or gunfire, call MPD's non-emergency line at 414-933-4444, or 911 in an emergency.

The rental platforms also take complaints: Airbnb and Vrbo both have neighbor-report forms, and Ald. Dimitrijevic's office says it reported the Burrell Street property to the platform directly.
